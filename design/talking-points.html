1 · What I chose to grow

Growth focus statement

Learn how connected data tracking works across watchOS and iOS — background execution, data ownership, transport reliability, source of truth — by producing one complete workout captured on the watch and reviewed on the phone, together with a log of what I predicted wrongly, so that I can build a data crossing that holds through screen-off and force-quit, and explain why it holds.

The log of wrong predictions was in the plan from day one.

I train HYROX: eight runs, eight stations, and the rest between them counts. I wanted an app that records the race on the watch and explains it honestly on the phone.

2 · My starting point

Zero.

HealthKit, workout sessions, background execution, moving data between two devices — I had used none of them.

So I did not plan to build first. I planned to predict first. Before every experiment I wrote what I expected; afterwards, what happened, and the gap between them.

Rule, stated at the top of the workbook: a prediction is never edited once the test has run. A wrong prediction is the evidence, not a mistake to hide. Git history shows each prediction committed before its result.

3 · What and how I learned

A rest of 2.03 seconds.

In Cycle 5 I stopped tapping through tests at a desk and ran a real session. Out of breath, pressing a button on my wrist between stations. At the second rest, I pressed it twice by accident.

3 · What the mistake taught me

Not all numbers are equally true.

The obvious split is recorded versus calculated. That split is wrong. The real one sits inside “recorded”.

MEASUREDThe device recorded it alone — heart rate, energy, total time
MARKEDA human pressed a button — every station boundary
DERIVEDWorked out from those — every station duration
UNSUPPORTEDThe data cannot answer it at all
ASSUMEDA fifth kind, in no plan of mine: the app printed “SkiErg” when I had done burpees — because it assumed, and never asked

My total time and my station start time are both timestamps and look identical on screen. One came from a sensor. The other came from a tired person pressing a button.

3 · The pattern underneath

A green signal hiding a failure. Seventeen times.

The signal saidWhat was actually true
Running normally79.4% of elapsed time was not being counted. No crash, no error.
AuthorisedRead permission was denied. HealthKit returns an empty set, never an error.
Build succeededMissing usage description. Instant termination, before any catch could run.
INTACTEvery offset was negative. The check tested completeness, never plausibility.
AGREESegment names matched. No durations were compared, and nothing said so.
MEASUREDApple’s own metadata: HKWeatherHumidity 4900 %.
All seventeen are indexed with workbook line numbers. Five were produced by instruments built in this project to catch exactly this. Two were my own reasoning errors, both retracted in place, with the wrong text kept above the retraction.
A passing test only proves what it actually checked.
3 · And then I checked Apple

0.333 seconds apart. Recorded without complaint.

I pressed the lap button in Apple’s own Workout app twice, as fast as I could. It stored both marks. No debounce, no flag, nothing indicating the second was suspect — the same failure as my 2.03-second roxzone.

Apple has not solved this either.

So the problem I hit is not inexperience. Marking boundaries accurately mid-effort is unsolved on the platform, and solving it properly is a real contribution rather than catching up.

4 · Shown live

Three clocks, one of them wrong.

During a workout the watch shows the same elapsed time computed three ways. Drop your wrist for a minute and one of them falls behind.

TicksCounts timer firings — drifts
DateTwo timestamps — exact
SystemRendered by watchOS — exact

That one screen is my entire first cycle happening in front of you. It is also the answer to the obvious objection: the percentages measure how often the app was allowed to run, not the accuracy of anything recorded. Every time in the race record is a subtraction between two timestamps.

And one line of metadata: there is no HYROX workout type in HealthKit, but Apple’s own Health app now displays my workout as HYROX — while I keep my own live screens.
